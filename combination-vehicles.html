CDL Combination Vehicles practice test

Required for a Class A CDL (tractor-trailers). The real test has 20 questions in most states. Pick an answer to see if you're right and why.

Study all 15 questions with answers
  1. What is the 'crack-the-whip' effect?
    Rearward amplification, where a quick steering move can tip over the trailer
    Sudden steering is amplified toward the back of a combination. The rear trailer can tip over even if the tractor stays upright.
  2. What color is the emergency (supply) air line glad hand?
    Red
    The emergency or supply line is red. The service line is blue.
  3. What color is the service air line glad hand?
    Blue
    The blue service line carries air controlled by the brake pedal or trailer hand brake to apply the trailer brakes.
  4. What does the trailer air supply control look like?
    A red, eight-sided knob
    Push the red eight-sided knob in to supply the trailer with air. Pull it out to shut off air and apply the trailer emergency brakes.
  5. What happens if the emergency line breaks or comes apart while driving?
    The trailer emergency brakes come on
    Loss of pressure in the emergency line causes the trailer's emergency (spring) brakes to apply.
  6. The tractor protection valve closes automatically when air pressure drops to:
    Between 20 and 45 psi
    The valve keeps air in the tractor if the trailer lines break or leak badly. It closes in the 20-45 psi range.
  7. Should you use the trailer hand valve (trolley valve) to park?
    No, use the parking brakes
    The hand valve uses service air, which can leak off and let the trailer roll. Always park with the spring parking brakes.
  8. When coupling, how high should the trailer be compared to the fifth wheel?
    Just low enough that the trailer is raised slightly when the tractor backs under it
    If the trailer is too high it may not couple right. Set it so the fifth wheel lifts it slightly as you back under.
  9. After coupling, what should you see when you look at the fifth wheel?
    No space between the upper and lower fifth wheel, and the jaws locked around the shank of the kingpin
    A gap means the trailer is riding on the jaws or is high-coupled. The jaws must close around the kingpin shank, not the head.
  10. How do you test that the coupling is secure?
    With the trailer brakes locked, pull forward gently in low gear (a tug test)
    A gentle tug against the locked trailer brakes confirms the kingpin is locked in the fifth wheel.
  11. Before driving away after coupling, the landing gear should be:
    Fully raised, with the crank handle secured
    Landing gear left partly down can catch on railroad tracks or other objects.
  12. During a turn, the trailer wheels follow a path that is:
    Shorter than the tractor wheels' path (off-tracking)
    Off-tracking means the rear wheels cut inside the path of the front wheels, which is why you need to turn wide.
  13. Your trailer starts to swing out in a jackknife skid. What should you do?
    Release the brakes to let the wheels grip again
    Trailer jackknifes usually come from locked trailer wheels. Releasing the brakes lets the tires regain traction.
  14. Compared to an empty rig, a fully loaded rig is about how much more likely to roll over in a crash?
    About 10 times more likely
    The high center of gravity of a loaded trailer makes rollovers far more likely. Steer gently and take curves slowly.
  15. Which statement about combination vehicles and rollovers is true?
    Keeping cargo low and steering gently reduces rollover risk
    Keep the load as close to the ground as possible and avoid quick lane changes or tight turns at speed.
