CDL General Knowledge practice test

Required for every CDL class. The real test has 50 questions in most states. Pick an answer to see if you're right and why.

Study all 30 questions with answers
  1. You drive a 40-foot truck at under 40 mph. What is the minimum following distance?
    4 seconds
    Below 40 mph, leave at least one second for every 10 feet of vehicle length. 40 feet means 4 seconds.
  2. You drive a 40-foot truck at 55 mph. What is the minimum following distance?
    5 seconds
    Above 40 mph, add one second to the one-second-per-10-feet rule. 4 seconds plus 1 equals 5.
  3. Total stopping distance is made up of which three parts?
    Perception distance, reaction distance and braking distance
    Total stopping distance is the distance you travel while you see the hazard, react to it and brake to a stop.
  4. About how long does it take an alert driver to react once they see a hazard?
    About three-quarters of a second
    The average alert driver takes about 3/4 of a second to react after seeing and recognizing a hazard.
  5. If you double your speed, your braking distance increases by about:
    Four times
    Braking distance grows with the square of speed. Double the speed and it takes roughly four times the distance to stop.
  6. Why does an empty truck often need more distance to stop than a loaded one?
    The brakes, tires and springs are designed for heavy loads, so an empty truck has less traction
    Brakes, tires, springs and shock absorbers work best when loaded. An empty truck can lock wheels and skid more easily.
  7. Hydroplaning can happen at speeds as low as:
    30 mph
    With enough water on the road, tires can lose contact with the surface at speeds as low as 30 mph.
  8. How far ahead should you look while driving?
    12 to 15 seconds ahead
    Looking 12 to 15 seconds ahead gives you time to spot hazards and change speed or lanes. At highway speed that is about a quarter mile.
  9. You stop on a two-lane road. Where do you place your rear warning triangle?
    About 100 feet behind the vehicle
    On a two-lane road, place one device within 10 feet of the vehicle on the traffic side, one about 100 feet behind and one about 100 feet ahead.
  10. You stop on a one-way or divided highway. Where do the warning devices go?
    10 feet, 100 feet and 200 feet behind the vehicle
    Traffic only approaches from behind on a divided or one-way road, so all three devices go behind: at 10, 100 and 200 feet.
  11. You stop just past a hill or curve. How far back can the rear warning device go?
    Up to 500 feet
    If a hill, curve or other obstruction blocks the view, move the rear device back as far as 500 feet so drivers get enough warning.
  12. What is the minimum tread depth for steering axle tires?
    4/32 inch
    Front (steering) tires need at least 4/32 inch of tread in every major groove. Other tires need at least 2/32 inch.
  13. Steering wheel free play is too much if it is more than about:
    10 degrees (about 2 inches on a 20-inch wheel)
    More than 10 degrees of play, roughly 2 inches of movement at the rim of a 20-inch wheel, makes the vehicle hard to steer.
  14. During a pre-trip inspection, how many missing leaves in a leaf spring put the vehicle out of service?
    One-fourth or more of the leaves
    A leaf spring with one-fourth or more of its leaves missing puts the vehicle out of service.
  15. What is the most common cause of serious skids?
    Driving too fast for conditions
    Most serious skids come from going too fast for road conditions. Over-braking, over-steering and over-accelerating also cause skids.
  16. Your drive wheels start to skid. What should you do first?
    Take your foot off the accelerator and stop braking
    To regain traction, let the rear wheels roll again: stop braking, ease off the accelerator, then steer where you want to go and be ready to countersteer.
  17. In an emergency, why is steering away from a hazard often better than stopping?
    You can usually turn to miss an obstacle more quickly than you can stop
    Top-heavy trucks need a lot of distance to stop. A quick turn often avoids the hazard in less space.
  18. If you must leave the road to avoid a crash, you should:
    Avoid braking until speed drops to about 20 mph, and keep one set of wheels on the pavement if you can
    Hard braking on a soft shoulder can cause a skid. Slow down gradually and keep one side on the pavement if possible.
  19. Your safe speed downhill is 40 mph. Using snub braking, when do you apply the brakes?
    At 45 mph, braking down to 35 mph
    When you reach 5 mph over your safe speed, brake firmly until you are 5 mph under it, then release. Repeat as needed.
  20. When should you choose a low gear for a long downgrade?
    Before you start down the grade
    Shifting down on a steep grade can be hard or impossible. Pick the right low gear before you start down.
  21. What is the legal blood alcohol limit for a CMV driver?
    0.04%
    Federal law sets the limit for commercial drivers at 0.04% blood alcohol concentration, half the usual limit for car drivers.
  22. Which fire extinguisher class is used on electrical fires?
    Class C
    Class C is for electrical fires. Class B is for flammable liquids like gasoline and grease. Most trucks carry a B:C extinguisher.
  23. When should you never remove the radiator cap?
    When the engine is hot
    The cooling system is under pressure when hot. Opening it can spray scalding coolant. Let the engine cool first.
  24. What is the minimum number of tiedowns for any flatbed load?
    Two, with at least one for every 10 feet of cargo
    You need at least one tiedown per 10 feet of cargo and never fewer than two, no matter how small the load.
  25. After you start a trip, when must you first check your cargo and its securement?
    Within the first 50 miles
    Check cargo within the first 50 miles, then every 3 hours or 150 miles, whichever comes first, and after every break.
  26. Objects in a convex (curved) mirror look:
    Smaller and farther away than they really are
    Convex mirrors show a wider area but make things look smaller and farther away. Keep that in mind when judging distance.
  27. At night, you should switch to low beams when an oncoming vehicle is within:
    500 feet
    Dim your lights within 500 feet of an oncoming vehicle and when following another vehicle so you don't blind the driver.
  28. When crossing railroad tracks, you should never:
    Shift gears while on the tracks
    Pick a gear that gets you all the way across before you start. Shifting on the tracks can stall the vehicle.
  29. When making a right turn, you should:
    Go straight ahead until the rear clears the curb, then turn, keeping the rear close to the curb
    Swinging left before a right turn invites cars to pass you on the right. Keep the back of the trailer near the curb so no one can squeeze in.
  30. When backing, it is safest to back toward:
    The driver's side
    Backing toward the driver's side lets you watch the rear of the vehicle by looking out the window. Use a helper whenever you can.
