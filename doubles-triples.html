CDL Doubles and Triples practice test

For the T endorsement, needed to pull two or three trailers. The real test has 20 questions in most states. Pick an answer to see if you're right and why.

Study all 10 questions with answers
  1. Which trailer in a set of triples is most likely to turn over?
    The last trailer
    Rearward amplification, the crack-the-whip effect, is strongest at the back of the combination.
  2. Where should the heaviest trailer go in a set of doubles?
    Directly behind the tractor
    Put the heavier trailer up front. A heavy rear trailer increases the risk of sway and rollover.
  3. What is a converter dolly?
    A coupling device with one or two axles and a fifth wheel used to connect a second trailer
    The converter dolly turns a semi-trailer into a full trailer so it can be pulled behind another trailer.
  4. Before coupling the second trailer, how do you keep it from moving?
    Make sure its spring brakes are set or chock the wheels
    If the second trailer doesn't have spring brakes, charge its air tanks and disconnect the emergency line to set its brakes, or chock it.
  5. After coupling doubles, how should the shut-off valves be set?
    Open on the first trailer, closed at the rear of the last trailer
    Open valves let air flow through to every trailer. The valves at the very rear must be closed so air doesn't escape.
  6. How can you check that air is flowing to all trailers?
    Open the emergency line shut-off valve at the back of the last trailer and listen for air escaping
    Air rushing out of the rear valve shows the whole system is charged. Close the valve again before driving.
  7. Compared to a single trailer, doubles and triples need:
    More following distance and more space to change lanes
    Longer combinations take more distance to stop and are less stable. Leave more room in every direction.
  8. What usually connects the converter dolly to the trailer in front of it?
    A pintle hook
    The dolly's eye attaches to a pintle hook at the back of the trailer ahead.
  9. Why should steering be extra smooth when pulling doubles or triples?
    Sudden movements are amplified at the rear trailer and can cause rollovers
    A quick lane change that feels minor in the tractor can whip the last trailer hard enough to flip it.
  10. When pulling doubles, you should check that the converter dolly air tank is:
    Drained of moisture and its petcock closed before driving
    Drain moisture from the dolly tank, then close the petcock so the dolly brakes can work.
