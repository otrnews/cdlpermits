CDL School Bus practice test

For the S endorsement, needed to drive a school bus. You'll also need the Passenger (P) endorsement. The real test has 20 questions in most states.

Study all 15 questions with answers
  1. How far out from the bus does the danger zone extend?
    Up to 30 feet on all sides
    The danger zone is the area on all sides of the bus where children are at greatest risk of being hit, by the bus or by passing traffic. It can extend up to 30 feet.
  2. Outside flat side mirrors should be adjusted so you can see about how far behind the bus?
    About 200 feet, or 4 bus lengths
    Set the flat mirrors so you can see about 200 feet (4 bus lengths) behind the bus along the sides.
  3. What are the crossover mirrors used for?
    Seeing the area directly in front of the bus and along the sides that you can't see from the seat
    Crossover mirrors show the blind area right in front of the bumper and the front of the bus, where small children can be hidden.
  4. What is the overhead inside mirror used for?
    Monitoring passenger activity inside the bus
    The inside overhead mirror lets you watch passengers without turning around.
  5. When approaching a stop, when should you turn on the alternating flashing amber lights?
    About 5 to 10 seconds before the stop, or as your state law requires
    The amber lights warn other drivers that you are about to stop. Turn them on early, usually about 5-10 seconds or 100-300 feet before the stop, depending on state law.
  6. When are the alternating flashing red lights used?
    When the bus is stopped to load or unload students
    Red lights and the stop arm tell traffic to stop while students get on or off.
  7. Students who must cross the road should walk how far in front of the bus?
    About 10 feet, where you can see them
    Students should cross about 10 feet in front of the bus so they stay in your direct line of sight.
  8. A student drops something near the bus. What should they do?
    Move away from the bus and ask the driver for help
    Bending down near the bus puts the student out of your sight. Tell students to never pick up dropped items and to come to you for help instead.
  9. Before pulling away from a stop, you should:
    Count the students and check all mirrors to make sure no one is near the bus
    Account for every student who got off. If you can't account for one, secure the bus and check around and under it.
  10. After an evacuation, students should go to a safe place at least how far from the road?
    100 feet off the road, toward oncoming traffic
    Moving toward oncoming traffic keeps students away from debris if another vehicle hits the bus.
  11. At a railroad crossing, a school bus should stop how far from the nearest rail?
    Between 15 and 50 feet
    Stop 15 to 50 feet from the tracks, then open the service door and your window and listen for a train.
  12. Before crossing railroad tracks, what should you do with the radio and other noisy equipment?
    Turn them off so you can hear a train
    Turn off radios and noisy equipment and ask students to be quiet so you can hear an approaching train.
  13. Your bus stalls on the railroad tracks. What should you do?
    Get everyone off right away and move them away from the tracks, toward the direction the train is coming from
    Evacuate immediately. Move students away from the tracks and toward the oncoming train so they aren't hit by debris after a collision.
  14. When a school bus turns, the rear can swing out up to:
    3 feet
    Tail swing of up to 3 feet means the rear corner can hit people or objects next to the bus when turning. Check mirrors before and during turns.
  15. If you leave the driver's seat while students are on board, you should:
    Secure the bus: set the parking brake, put it in park or neutral, turn off the engine and remove the key
    Never leave a bus with students aboard where it could be moved or rolled. Secure it completely first.
